ScreenGym — Terms & Privacy

Effective date: 05 October 2026

Where your data lives

Everything ScreenGym shows you is stored in a local database on this device only. There is no server, no account required, and ScreenGym never sends your data anywhere, including to us. The one exception is the optional Google backup described below — off unless you turn it on yourself.

Backup and restore (optional, off by default)

In Account, you can turn on “Back up history to Google.” If you do, only your local usage-history database is copied to your own Google account's backup storage — nothing else about the app or your phone. This lets your history come back automatically if you reinstall ScreenGym on a new phone signed into that same Google account. It's entirely optional and off unless you explicitly turn it on, and once it leaves this device it's governed by Google's own backup security terms, not this document. Your Premium status, if any, is separate from this toggle — that's tied to your Google account through Play Billing and follows you regardless of whether backup is on.

What's collected, and how

ScreenGym reads two things Android itself already tracks at the operating-system level: which apps you open and for how long (via Android's Usage Access permission), and how much mobile and Wi-Fi data each app uses (via Android's network usage statistics). ScreenGym does not read message content, browsing history, photos, contacts, or anything typed on the screen — only the fact that an app was open, when, and how much data it used.

What's derived from that data

From those two raw signals, ScreenGym computes daily summaries — active/passive time, unlock counts, category breakdowns, network speed estimates, and a persona/addiction score — and stores those summaries so you can see trends over time. All of this computation happens on your device; none of it is sent elsewhere.

How long it's kept

On install, ScreenGym can only see up to the last 30 days of history — a limit set by how long Android's own usage log retains data, not by ScreenGym. ScreenGym aligns all data so that every part of the app reflects the same history window, rather than some sections covering more days than others.

From there, ScreenGym keeps building and storing your history indefinitely — nothing is ever automatically deleted, regardless of your plan. History can grow to span multiple years of use.

What differs by plan is what you can see, not what's kept. Free accounts can view up to the most recent 7 days of history. Premium accounts can view the full history that's been collected, however far back it goes. If you upgrade from free to Premium, nothing is lost in the meantime — you'll immediately be able to see everything ScreenGym already collected while you were on the free plan, not just data going forward.

Required permission

ScreenGym needs Android's “Usage Access” permission to function at all — without it, the app can't see which apps you've used. This is a standard Android permission, granted manually in Settings, and can be revoked at any time (ScreenGym will simply stop collecting new data until it's granted again).

If you uninstall

Uninstalling ScreenGym deletes its local database along with it, on this device. If you never turned on Google backup, there's nothing left anywhere after that. If you did turn it on, a copy remains in your Google account's backup storage until it's naturally replaced by a newer backup or you clear it yourself through your Google account's own backup settings — ScreenGym has no way to reach back and delete that copy once it exists.

Accuracy of data and estimates

ScreenGym measures and estimates your screen time, network usage, and related figures using Android's own built-in usage and network APIs. These are estimates, not guaranteed-accurate measurements — Android's own reporting can lag, differ slightly between screens, or be affected by how the operating system batches and reports usage data. For example, screen time only counts foreground use, while the screen is actively being looked at — it does not count background activity where the screen is assumed not to be in active use, such as Chromecasting or playing audio over Bluetooth. Persona, Addiction Level, network speed estimates, and Weekly Analysis are all derived from these same underlying estimates and carry the same limitations. To the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, ScreenGym and its developer are not liable for any decision made, or any loss or inconvenience arising, from inaccuracies, delays, or discrepancies in this data.

Notifications

ScreenGym's notifications — goal alerts and the weekly analysis reminder — depend on Android's own background-processing and notification systems, which can delay, batch, or suppress notifications depending on your device's battery settings, Do Not Disturb mode, notification permissions, or manufacturer-specific power management. ScreenGym does not guarantee that any notification will arrive at a specific time, or at all. Notifications are informational only and must not be relied on for time-sensitive or safety-related purposes.

Persona, Addiction Level, and fitness-related terminology are not medical advice

The Persona and Addiction Level features, and the fitness-themed language used throughout the app, are simplified, self-reflection tools based on rule-based thresholds applied to your own usage data — not a clinical, psychological, or medical assessment of behavioral addiction, fitness, or any other condition. They're provided for informational and entertainment purposes only. ScreenGym is not a healthcare product and should not be used as a substitute for professional advice, diagnosis, or treatment. If you have concerns about your relationship with your phone or technology use, please consult a qualified professional.

App categories are informal, not evaluative

Mind Nutrients groups your apps into four informal categories (Carbohydrates, Protein, Fats, Fiber), plus an Other catch-all, as a lightweight, food-themed way to describe HOW an app is typically used — for example, short-form video versus messaging versus reading — not a judgment of the app, its publisher, or the time you spend in it. The grouping is a simplification and will not fit every app or every person's use of it perfectly; it isn't intended to label any app, developer, or user negatively. If you'd rather see your usage without this framing, the Top 5 experiences and per-app breakdowns in Network and Screen Steps show the same underlying data by app, without any category attached.
